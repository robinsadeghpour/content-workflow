## Writing Style Rules

**Casual and human:**
- write mostly lowercase (not "I Was Skeptical" but "i was skeptical")
- sound like texting a friend
- not formal, not salesy
- would you actually say this out loud?

**Postel positioning:**
- tool/multiplier, not the hero
- mentioned naturally, not forced
- educational value first, product second

**Must pass Humanizer check:**
- no AI vocabulary (leverage, delve, unlock, etc.)
- no inflated symbolism
- no promotional language
- casual, authentic voice

## The 6-Slide Story Arc

Every viral slideshow follows this exact structure:

### Slide 1: HOOK
**Formula:** [Person] + [showed them AI/tool/result]

**Purpose:** Grab attention, introduce the "other person"

**Examples:**
- "I showed my Nan what AI thinks her living room could look like"
- "I showed my investor what one tweet did for my business"
- "I showed my mentor what happened after I started posting on X"

**Photo selection:** Eye-catching, represents "showing someone" (collaboration photo, workspace, or hook visual)

**Writing style:**
- mostly lowercase (casual, human)
- like talking to a friend
- not salesy or formal

---

### Slide 2: CONFLICT
**Formula:** [Why it was needed / the doubt / the problem]

**Purpose:** Establish the conflict or skepticism

**Examples:**
- "She hasn't changed it since the 90s"
- "He said social media was a waste of time"
- "He told me to focus on 'real marketing'"

**Photo selection:** Grind/struggle photos (late night coding, intense focus, workspace showing the hard work)

---

### Slide 3: SOLUTION
**Formula:** [The tool/method that solved it]

**Purpose:** Introduce the solution (your product/tool)

**Examples:**
- "So I showed her this app"
- "So I showed him this AI writing tool"
- "Then I found Postel's AI writer"

**Photo selection:** Creator setup with tools visible (RODE mic, dual monitors, professional workspace)

---

### Slide 4: TURNING POINT
**Formula:** [The moment it clicks — varies by post, do NOT use "Wait..." every time]

**Purpose:** The pivot — resistance breaks, results land, something shifts

**Rotate through these approaches (pick what fits the story):**

1. Quoted surprise — "wait... it actually sounds like me?"
   (Use occasionally, not every post — it's now a cliché if overused)

2. Implied reaction — "he went quiet for a second"
   (More subtle, feels more real)

3. Numbers drop — "7 days later. 3k followers."
   (Skip the emotion, just land the result hard)

4. First-person realization — "that's when it clicked"
   (Works for vulnerable/honest formats)

5. Contrast statement — "he'd been overthinking it for months. took 10 minutes."
   (No quote, just irony — very punchy)

6. Skip it — go straight from solution (slide 3) to result (slide 5)
   (Works when the story flows without needing a reaction beat)

**Rule:** vary the turning point style across posts. Same formula every time = templated, scrolled past.

**Photo selection:** Happy/surprised/positive moments (smiling, rooftop happiness, milestone celebrations)

---

### Slide 5: RESULT
**Formula:** [Real-world outcome / action taken]

**Purpose:** Show the tangible result of the transformation

**Examples:**
- "Now she wants to go to John Lewis this weekend"
- "Now he wants to invest in my next product"
- "Now he asks me for content advice"

**Photo selection:** **POWER SHOT - Ultimate payoff** (paradise/freedom visual like photo-23's infinity pool, or happy achievement moment)

---

### Slide 6: CTA
**Formula:** [Invitation for viewer to try it too]

**Purpose:** Convert the viewer to try your product

**Examples:**
- "Download Snugly and show your nan too"
- "Try Postel and prove the doubters wrong too"
- "Get Postel and become the expert"

**Photo selection:** Professional but achievable workspace (shows "you can do this too")

## The Hook Formula (Weight: 10.0)

```
[Another person] + [conflict/doubt] → showed them [result] → mind changed
```

### Key Principles:

1. **Focus on ANOTHER PERSON** - Not yourself
   - ✅ "I showed my investor..."
   - ❌ "I did X for my business..."

2. **Include CONFLICT or DOUBT** - There must be resistance
   - ✅ "He said it was a waste of time"
   - ❌ "He liked the idea"

3. **Show the TRANSFORMATION** - Mind changed by evidence
   - ✅ "Now he wants to invest"
   - ❌ "He was impressed"

4. **Make it RELATABLE** - Common skeptic types
   - Investor who doubted
   - Mentor who dismissed
   - Client who hesitated
   - Co-founder who was skeptical
   - Family member who didn't understand

## Storytelling Psychology

Reference: `.claude/skills/writing/data/storytelling-framework.md` for full framework.

**Apply these to every slideshow:**

1. **Address one person** — Write slide text as if talking to one friend, not an audience. "you" not "you guys."

2. **"You" for desires, "I" for problems:**
   - Slide 1-2 (hook/conflict): "I" language — "i showed my investor...", "i was stuck at..."
   - Slide 5-6 (result/CTA): "you" language — "now you can...", "try this yourself"

3. **Open loops in captions** — Start a thought on one slide, resolve it 2 slides later. Example: Slide 2 mentions "one decision changed everything" → Slide 4 reveals what the decision was.

4. **Re-hooks between slides** — If the story sags at slide 3-4, add a micro-hook: "but here's where it gets interesting", "and that's not even the best part"

5. **Specificity over generality** — "went from 0 to $44K in 8 months" beats "grew my business." Name the method, name the number, name the time.

## Format 3: Value Tips Carousel

Pure educational value, no character/story. Robin's real numbers give it credibility.

Structure (6 slides):
- Slide 1: Hook — curiosity gap or bold claim based on real results
  - "3 things that got me to 9M impressions on X"
  - "why your X posts get ignored (and how I fixed it)"
  - "the thing no one tells you about growing on X"
- Slides 2-5: One tip per slide — specific, actionable, short
  - lead with the insight, not the explanation
  - use real numbers where possible (9M impressions, $200/month ad revenue, etc.)
  - casual tone, like advice from a friend not a course
- Slide 6: Soft CTA — "looking to grow on X? try postel" or "postel.app — AI that writes in your voice"

Rules:
- Hook must create genuine curiosity or promise specific value (not vague "tips for growth")
- Each tip slide = one clear idea, 1-2 sentences max
- No story, no character, no conflict — just the insight
- CTA is soft and natural, not a hard sell
- Rotate this format in with story arc posts — don't use two tips posts in a row

Tip slide formula (from Robin's edits Feb 19):
- **Frequency over duration** — "3+ times a day" beats "30 days straight." give something actionable today, not a long commitment
- **Extend the metaphor** — if you open with an analogy, keep it going. "lottery ticket → buy more tickets" sticks better than switching frames mid-tip
- **Add the consequence** — not just "do X" but "do X → here's what happens (algo can't ignore you)"
- **Cross-platform language** — TikTok audience consumes YouTube, TikToks, podcasts, not just X. repurpose tips should reference ALL content they have or consume, not just X threads

Good tip topics (based on Robin's actual experience):
- consistency > virality (posting daily > chasing one big tweet)
- reply guy method doesn't scale (effort without leverage)
- your voice is the differentiator (AI that sounds like you vs generic)
- repurposing is the cheat code (one idea → 5 platforms)
- X pays you to post ($200/month ad revenue at 8.5k followers)
- study what went viral, then adapt it (not copy)

## Caption Writing — Lessons from Robin's Edits (Feb 17)

When Robin edited the vulnerable/honest post description, the changes revealed 3 rules that now apply to all captions:

### Rule 1: Use insider terms the audience already knows
**Bad:** "i tried growing on twitter but nothing worked"
**Good:** "tried the reply guy method which took hours… and nothing worked"

"Reply guy method" is something the exact target audience (indie hackers, X growers) knows. When they read it, they think "oh that's me." Vague descriptions of failure don't create that moment.

**Apply to captions:** Name the specific strategy/method they tried and failed at. Don't just say "tried everything."

---

### Rule 2: Show you tried the *recommended* things and still failed
The reader needs to see that effort alone wasn't enough — otherwise the tool looks like a lazy shortcut.

**Structure:** tried X (which people say works) → it didn't work → *then* found the tool

This makes the product the key insight, not a crutch. It validates the reader's experience ("I also tried the right things and still failed").

---

### Rule 3: Name the process pain, not the outcome pain
**Bad:** "i was a bad writer" (outcome — too abstract)
**Good:** "i suck at marketing" or "overthought every tweet" (process — what it feels like in the moment)

Builders identify as people who suck at marketing, not people who are bad writers. The pain is in the daily experience of overthinking and deleting, not in the abstract label of "bad writer."

Frame captions around: what does the audience feel *while doing* the painful thing, not how they label themselves after.

---

### Summary (add these to every caption review):
- [ ] Did you name a specific method/strategy they tried? (insider language)
- [ ] Does it show effort-not-enough before the tool? (not lazy shortcut)
- [ ] Is the pain in the process, not the label? (overthinking vs "bad writer")

## What DOESN'T Work (Retired Patterns)

### ❌ Self-Focused Hooks
**Pattern:** Talking about yourself, not others' reactions

**Failed examples (Larry's tests):**
- "Why does my flat look like a student loan" → 905 views
- "See your room in 12+ styles before you commit" → 879 views

**Why it failed:** No other person, no conflict, no transformation story

**Weight:** 0 (retired)

---

### ❌ Feature Showcases
**Pattern:** Listing product features without emotional story

**Failed examples:**
- "See our app's features" → ~1K views
- Product demo videos
- Feature comparison slideshows

**Why it failed:** People don't care about features, they care about transformations

**Weight:** 0 (retired)

---

### ❌ Price Comparisons
**Pattern:** Comparing price points or value tiers

**Failed examples:**
- "The difference between $500 and $5000 taste" → 2,671 views

**Why it failed:** Self-focused, no human moment, no conflict resolution

**Weight:** 0 (retired)

---

### ❌ Vague Hooks
**Pattern:** Generic statements without specific person or conflict

**Failed examples:**
- "Want to redesign your room?"
- "Struggling with social media?"
- "Check out this tool"

**Why it failed:** No story, no emotional hook, just questions

**Weight:** 0 (retired)

## Caption Formula

Captions should:
1. **Reinforce the story** (not repeat it)
2. **Mention product naturally** (not forced)
3. **Use max 5 hashtags** (TikTok's current limit)
4. **Include CTA** (link in bio, app name, etc.)

**Example caption for Investor Story:**

```
My investor didn't believe in X until I showed him the Stripe dashboard 💰

One year of posting with @Postel's AI writer:
• 1.4M impressions in one day
• €44K total revenue
• 2 bootstrapped products

Now he's asking how to grow HIS audience 😂

Try it yourself → Postel.app

#indiehacker #buildinpublic #twitter #contentmarketing #saas
```

**Key elements:**
- ✅ Reinforces the transformation ("didn't believe → now asks")
- ✅ Includes specific metrics (social proof)
- ✅ Natural product mention (@Postel's AI writer)
- ✅ Exactly 5 hashtags
- ✅ Clear CTA (Postel.app)
- ✅ Emoji for visual break (not overused)

## Story Arc Emotional Progression

The 6 slides should follow an emotional journey:

```
Slide 1 (Hook):     CURIOSITY - "Who? What?"
Slide 2 (Conflict): TENSION - "Uh oh, resistance"
Slide 3 (Solution): HOPE - "Okay, there's a way..."
Slide 4 (Reaction): SURPRISE - "Wait, what?!"
Slide 5 (Result):   SATISFACTION - "That's amazing!"
Slide 6 (CTA):      DESIRE - "I want this too"
```

**Photo selection should match this progression:**
- Curiosity → Interesting visual hook
- Tension → Dark/grind aesthetic
- Hope → Professional/capable setup
- Surprise → Happy/bright moment
- Satisfaction → Paradise/achievement
- Desire → Achievable goal visual
