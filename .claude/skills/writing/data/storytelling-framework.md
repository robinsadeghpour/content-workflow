# Storytelling Framework

Reference guide for content generation. Read this before structuring any piece of content.

---

## 1. 5-Part Story Structure

Every piece of content — slides, LinkedIn post, caption — follows this arc:

**Hook (first line / first 5 seconds)**
Stop the scroll. One specific, curious, or surprising statement. No greetings, no preamble.
- Good: "This mistake cost me $12K." / "I tried this for 30 days and nearly quit."
- Bad: "Hey everyone, today I want to talk about..."

**Context (setup)**
Who, what, when. 1-2 sentences max. Just enough to understand the stakes.
- Good: "I'd been posting on X for 6 months. Nothing was working."

**Tension (the conflict)**
The problem, doubt, or resistance. This is where engagement lives — don't rush past it.
- "I tried the reply guy method. Hours every day. Zero traction."
- Let the tension breathe. Don't pivot too fast.

**Pivot (the turn)**
The moment everything shifts. Signal it clearly.
- "But here's the real reason it wasn't working."
- "That's when it clicked."
- "He went quiet for a second." (implied pivot)

**Payoff (resolution + CTA)**
The result, the lesson, the invitation. Be specific.
- "Went from 200 to 9M impressions in 8 months."
- Soft CTA: "Try it yourself." / "What would you do?" — never aggressive.

---

## 2. Psychology Techniques

**Address ONE person, never "hey guys"**
Write as if talking to one friend. "Hey guys" diffuses the personal connection that stops the scroll.

**"You" for desires, "I" for problems**
- Hook/conflict: "I" language — "I was stuck at $0 MRR for months..."
- Result/CTA: "You" language — "Now you can skip the part where I failed."
- This makes you a friend sharing experience, not a guru delivering advice.

**"Us vs problem" framing**
- "We weren't taught how to build in public" builds community.
- Never "you vs them" — it divides and alienates.

**Text tells the story, visuals carry the emotion**
Like a children's book: the words advance the plot, the image creates the feeling.
Match photo selection to the emotional beat of each slide (curiosity → tension → hope → surprise → satisfaction).

---

## 3. Attention Retention Techniques

**Re-hooks** — mid-content hooks that reset attention when it might sag
- "But here's where it gets interesting."
- "And that's not even the best part."
- Use at slide 3-4 or paragraph 3 if post is long.

**Open loops (Ovsiankina Effect)** — start a thought, delay the resolution
- "I made a decision that day. More on that in a second."
- "One thing changed everything. I'll get to it."
- The brain compulsively follows unresolved threads.

**Cause-and-effect chains** — keep the viewer following the logic
- "I did X → Because Y → Got Z"
- Chains are harder to exit than isolated statements.

**Pivot every ~5 seconds (video) / every 2-3 lines (text)**
Change tone, introduce new element, drop a number, shift perspective.
Flat content = passive watching → swipe.

---

## 4. Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Lack of specificity** — "I grew my business" vs "I went from 0 to $44K in 8 months"
- **Empty greetings** — waste the hook position, the most valuable real estate
- **Rushing the payoff** — let tension build before resolving; earn the resolution
- **Too much information** — one idea per piece of content. Cut everything else.
- **No emotion** — facts without feeling don't stick. Name the process pain, not the outcome label.
- **Vague hooks** — "I learned a lot" tells the viewer nothing. Be specific and unexpected.
